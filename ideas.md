# The Common Index — Design Direction

## Three directions considered

### Theme Name: The Reading Room
Very brief intro: Warm paper, black ink, and a muted red editorial accent create the feeling of an independent literary journal. The interface is quiet, tactile, and built around careful reading.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: The Observatory
Very brief intro: A dark, midnight-blue research interface with precise lines and luminous data marks frames ideas as signals moving across disciplines. It feels analytical and contemporary.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Marginalia
Very brief intro: A pale canvas with handwritten annotations, offset rules, and playful archival stamps gives the platform an intimate workshop character. It feels curious and human.
Probability: 0.08

## Chosen direction: The Reading Room

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial modernism with references to independent literary journals, museum catalogues, and archival reading rooms.

### Core Principles
1. **Quiet authority:** let typography, proportion, and editorial hierarchy carry the experience rather than decoration.
2. **Asymmetric attention:** use offset columns, rules, and margin notes to make the page feel composed rather than templated.
3. **Paper as atmosphere:** warm ivory, subtle grain, and restrained contrast should suggest a desk covered in meaningful work.
4. **Open inquiry:** language should invite serious thought without sounding institutional or gatekeeping.

### Color Philosophy
The base is warm ivory rather than pure white, evoking paper and making long-form content easier on the eye. Ink-black creates authority and legibility. A single vermilion accent signals action, annotation, and editorial intervention; it should appear sparingly enough to remain ownable.

### Layout Paradigm
A long-form editorial canvas with a narrow masthead, an oversized left-aligned thesis statement, a photographic still life offset to the right, and a lower section that behaves like an index card catalogue. Avoid perfectly centered hero content and avoid repeating identical cards.

### Signature Elements
- Hairline rules and short vermilion registration marks.
- Small uppercase metadata labels with generous tracking.
- A bracket-and-point brand symbol suggesting an opening, a citation, or an idea entering the record.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like turning a page or marking a passage: quick, precise, and low-drama. Hover states reveal a vermilion rule or nudge an arrow rather than producing large effects. Submission is always one clear email action.

### Animation
Use a restrained first-load reveal: masthead, thesis, and hero image enter in a staggered 40–70ms cadence. Hover transitions remain under 220ms and animate only opacity, color, and transform. Respect reduced motion and keep the page calm.

### Typography System
Use **DM Serif Display** for the masthead and large thesis; pair it with **IBM Plex Sans** for navigation, metadata, buttons, and body copy. Display headings should be sentence case, generous in line-height, and never over-weighted. Metadata is uppercase with 0.16em tracking.

### Brand Essence
The Common Index is a public-minded publishing room for people who think across disciplines, offering a considered place to share original papers and follow ideas as they travel.
Personality: **curious, exacting, generous**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and declarative. CTAs are direct but not transactional. Microcopy sounds like an editor speaking to a thoughtful peer.

Example lines:
- “Ideas worth keeping in circulation.”
- “Send the work. We’ll read it closely.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use “The Common Index” as a refined serif wordmark with a compact bracket-and-point symbol as its mark. The symbol should stand alone in the header and favicon; it should feel like a visual citation mark rather than a generic abstract logo.

### Signature Brand Color
Vermilion ink: `#C8472C`.
